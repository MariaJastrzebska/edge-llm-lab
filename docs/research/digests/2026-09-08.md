## New items

**It's Not the Size: Harness Design Determines Operational Stability in
Small Language Models** (arXiv 2605.12129) — **CITE, high priority.** This
is the closest-yet paper to bigos's own founding thesis, stated almost
identically in the title. Tests three harness conditions (model-only,
minimal-shell, 4-stage plan→execute→verify→recover pipeline) on three SLMs
(Gemma4 E2B, Qwen3.5:2B, Llama 3.2 3B) across 24 tasks, measuring Task
Success Rate / Valid TSR; finds the pipeline harness pushes Gemma4 E2B to
TSR=0.952, and a non-monotonic effect (minimal-shell TSR < model-only TSR)
in two of three models — harness quality dominates parameter count within
its scope. **Not competitive with the systems contribution bigos plans**:
it's an evaluation/prompting-level study (three fixed harness *shapes*,
task-success accuracy only) — no edge hardware, no energy/latency
telemetry, no runtime/OS-level mechanism, nothing resembling G1's
mask-cost/pipelining angle. It validates the premise bigos is built on
without touching the low-level-systems bar Maria requires for her own
contribution. **Action for whoever drafts the harness-OS motivation
section: open with this paper, then pivot immediately to "but nobody
measures *why*, in Joules and milliseconds, on real edge hardware, at the
mechanism level" — that's the differentiation bigos needs stated
explicitly.**

**HARBOR: Automated Harness Optimization** (arXiv 2604.20938, Apr 2026) —
differentiate-from. Formalizes long-horizon LLM-agent harness configuration
(context compaction, tool caching, semantic memory, trajectory reuse,
speculative tool prediction) as constrained noisy Bayesian optimization over
a mixed-variable config space; argues harness design is a first-class ML
problem once the flag space is large. Not SLM- or edge-specific — the
"harness" here is agent-orchestration plumbing for general-size models in
cloud/sandboxed execution, and the contribution is an automated search
algorithm (BayesOpt), not a runtime/OS layer or a hardware-cost mechanism.
Naming-adjacent risk worth flagging: "Harbor" is a crowded 2026 term (see
below), not a scientific overlap.

**EffGen: Enabling Small Language Models as Capable Autonomous Agents**
(arXiv 2602.00887) — differentiate-from. Open-source agentic framework for
local SLM deployment: prompt-optimization compression (57% avg token
reduction), task decomposition, complexity-based routing, unified
short/long-term/vector memory. Genuinely "harness for SLMs" in spirit and
open-source (PyPI `effgen`), but application/agent-orchestration layer, not
systems/runtime — no edge-hardware energy or latency measurement, no
kernel/scheduler/memory-management mechanism, no grammar/constrained-
decoding angle. Worth a related-work sentence; not a threat to G1's
mechanism-level claim.

**Better Harnesses, Smaller Models: Building 90% Cheaper Agents via
Automated Harness Adaptation** (arXiv 2607.08938) — differentiate-from.
Another entrant in the same "harness lets you use a smaller/cheaper model"
family — automated harness *adaptation* claiming SLM agents match LLM
performance at ~90% lower cost. Cost/agent-performance framing, not
systems/edge-hardware framing; no telemetry or mechanism-level contribution.
Reinforces the same point as 2605.12129: the qualitative claim "harness
beats model size" is becoming a small but real 2026 literature cluster —
now three independent papers (2605.12129, 2602.00887, 2607.08938) plus the
Bayesian-optimization framing of HARBOR. None compete on mechanism; all of
them make the motivation section's job easier and the differentiation
requirement stricter.

**Cluster, Route, Escalate: Cascaded Framework for Cost-Aware LLM Serving**
(arXiv 2606.27457) and **Is Escalation Worth It? A Decision-Theoretic
Characterization of LLM Cascades** (arXiv 2605.06350) — differentiate-from/
cite, filed together as the "escalation" angle Maria named. Both address
small-model-first, escalate-to-large-model-on-failure cascade routing
(cost-aware serving, cloud-side). Relevant if bigos's harness design
includes an escalation/cascade feature (SLM handles the common case, a
bigger model or cloud call handles the hard tail) — citable prior art for
that specific harness component, not competitive with G1's decode-time
mask-cost mechanism. No edge/on-device angle in either (cloud multi-model
serving).

**small-model-harness** (PyPI package, `pypi.org/project/small-model-harness/`)
— differentiate-from, filed as a naming/crowding data point rather than a
paper. Open-source, MIT-licensed: "session-level intelligence for small LLMs
(2B–4B)... compensates for tool-calling failures deterministically, no
extra LLM calls, no cloud dependency," citing an internal claim that a
4-stage scaffold takes a 2B model from 58%→95% task success (same shape of
claim as 2605.12129, unclear if it's the same underlying work or an
independent reimplementation). Not a research contribution to compete with,
but confirms "harness for small models" is *also* an active open-source
practitioner space, not just an academic one — the space bigos enters is
more crowded on branding than on rigorous edge-hardware measurement.

**tinyedge-runtime** (PyPI, `pypi.org/project/tinyedge-runtime/`) —
differentiate-from. Apache-2.0 "execution kernel and strategy SDK for
running model-driven workloads on edge devices and robots" — the closest
name-and-scope match to bigos's "harness OS ... edge ... robots" framing of
anything found this iteration. Explicitly and deliberately scoped narrow,
though: standard-library-only, device-free, and its own docs disclaim
owning "hardware drivers, benchmark campaigns," or proving "model quality,
deadline performance, ... benchmark success" — i.e. it is a contracts/
lifecycle layer, not a measurement or research harness. This is reassuring
rather than threatening: it occupies the exact adjacent niche bigos would
need to either build on or explicitly position against, and it explicitly
does *not* do the telemetry/benchmarking work that is bigos's actual value-add.

**HARNESS-LM: A Three-Phase Training Recipe for Harnessing SLMs in

application (ads/search retrieval), a training recipe not a runtime, no
edge or systems angle. Filed only to avoid re-discovery.

## Verdict summary

Zero direct scoops of G1's mechanism (mask-cost tax, shadow-mask
pipelining) or of a low-level-systems edge-telemetry contribution. But the
umbrella claim itself — "a good harness matters more than raw model size
for small LLMs" — is **not novel as a bare statement**: at least three 2026
papers and two-plus open-source packages already say some version of it.
This is not a kill-criterion scoop (none combine it with edge-hardware
energy/latency measurement, a pluggable low-level mechanism module, or
robotics/medical framing), but it sharpens what the bigos paper's
contribution actually has to be: not "harness > model size" (taken), but
the specific low-level mechanisms (grammar-mask pipelining, memory/kernel-
level SLM adaptations, rigorous Joules/ms measurement on real edge SoCs)
that make a harness *provably* cheaper or faster, which nothing found this
iteration does. Not a tier-1 event — no new `NEEDS_INPUT.md` gate opened.
