### Part 1 — vocab-size hypothesis: public-spec rank-order check (research-only, no hardware)

STATUS's Next Action item 3 asked for a literature-only check of whether public
`n_vocab` figures rank-order with 005's 28× mask-cost spread. Pulled config specs
(HF model cards / GitHub issues, since `huggingface.co` direct fetch is blocked by
this session's egress policy — used indexed search-result snippets instead):

| model | vocab_size | 005 tax |
|---|---|---|
| llama3.2:1b | 128,256 | +6.15% |
| qwen2.5:0.5b | 151,936 | +1.84% |
| qwen3:0.6b | 151,936 (config; ~151,669 actual tokenizer, known off-by-padding issue) | +2.42% |
| gemma3:1b | 262,144 | +30.36% |

**Result: does NOT cleanly rank-order.** Gemma3's case (largest vocab, largest tax
by far) is consistent with the hypothesis. But llama3.2:1b has the *smallest*
vocab of the four and the *second-highest* tax — higher than both Qwen models,
which have a ~19% larger vocab than llama3.2 yet a 2.5–3.3× *smaller* tax. And the
two Qwen models share effectively the same vocab_size (151,936) but differ in tax
by 32% relative (1.84% vs 2.42%) — same vocab, different tax, so vocab size can't
be the whole story even within a matched pair.

**Verdict on the hypothesis: weakened, not confirmed.** Vocab size predicts the top
of the range (gemma3) but is contradicted by the bottom three (llama3.2 vs. both
Qwens). This downgrades "vocab size" in `NEEDS_INPUT.md` option 2 from "plausible
leading candidate" to "explains at most 1 of 4 data points, needs a co-driver or a
different explanation entirely" — added as an update note to `NEEDS_INPUT.md` since
it materially affects that option's premise. Not a new tier-1 event on its own (no
new hardware result, no scoop, no deadline change) — folded into the existing gate
rather than opening a new one.

No hardware time spent; this was public-spec desk research only, as instructed.

### Part 2 — literature sweep (scoop-check top-up + general query)

Re-ran the standard PSC / GRID / Attention-Meets-Reachability query plus a general
grammar-constrained-decoding sweep. Two genuinely new items (not in `seen.json`):

1. **["Future Validity is the Missing Statistic: From Impossibility to
   Φ-Estimation for Grammar-Faithful Speculative Decoding"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07698)**
   — shows that speculative decoding combined with local grammar-mask access samples
   from the *wrong* distribution (TV gap up to 0.996 on Dyck grammars w/ Qwen3-8B
   in their setup) unless corrected by a "future validity" statistic Φ. This is a
   **correctness-of-sampling** paper (speculative decoding + grammar), not a
   latency/mask-cost paper — different mechanism from G1 entirely, and speculative
   decoding isn't in scope for the sub-1B single-model regime G1 targets.
   **Verdict: differentiate-from.** Not a scoop; worth one related-work sentence at
   most if G1's paper ever discusses speculative decoding as an alternative
   mitigation.

2. **["The Parser Already Knows: Lightweight Bias Correction in Constrained
   Decoding"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10137)** (submitted 2026-08-10, Özgü/Wu/Van
   den Broeck/Kim) — argues rigid grammar masking distorts the LM's probability
   distribution toward valid-but-suboptimal outputs, and proposes a lightweight
   offline-trained logit correction using parser/lexer state to restore the true
   distribution cheaply (avoiding expensive online resampling). **Relevance: this
   is the same family of concern as the correctness-tax gap already flagged in
   `NEEDS_INPUT.md`** — independent confirmation from a different angle (distortion
   toward suboptimal-but-valid, not just wrong-but-valid) that schema/grammar
   validity and output quality are separable axes, reinforcing why 005/006's
   validity-only grading is a real gap and not a one-off finding from the
   Constraint Tax paper alone. **Verdict: cite** — good citation once the paper
   draft exists; also a candidate related-work anchor for the correctness-tax
   thread.
