**General sweep — algorithmic mask/grammar-constrained-decoding-speedup
cluster** (continuing the "avoid repeating the same query two iterations
running" instruction; this iteration re-anchored on the cluster named in
STATUS.md's "Known competitive pressure" section rather than the Apple-
Silicon-engine angle opened in iter 11, to check whether any recent entrant
has moved onto edge hardware). Three new items surfaced:

1. **Pre³: Enabling Deterministic Pushdown Automata for Faster Structured LLM
   Generation** (arXiv 2506.03887, ACL 2025) — precomputes prefix-conditioned
   edges to turn LR(1) grammar parsing into a deterministic pushdown automaton,
   removing runtime path-exploration overhead from constrained decoding.
   Explicitly targets **large-batch cloud serving** ("especially inefficient
   under large inference batches") — no edge hardware, no Joules, no
   single-stream/short-forward-pass measurement, the exact profile that keeps
   this cluster non-overlapping with G1/Route A. **Verdict: differentiate-from**
   — add to the mask-speedup cluster list (joins PSC, GRID,
   Attention-Meets-Reachability, Token-Space-Compression) as a specific,
   citable "here's the batch-serving-side answer to the same masking-overhead
   problem, and here's why it doesn't transfer to edge" reference.
2. **XGrammar-2: Dynamic and Efficient Structured Generation Engine for
   Agentic LLMs** (arXiv 2601.04426, AI & Agentic Systems 2026) — successor to
   XGrammar, adds tag-triggered structure switching and cross-request grammar
   caching for dynamic/agentic tool-calling workloads; claims >6× faster
   compilation and "near-zero end-to-end overhead" inside SGLang/vLLM serving.
   Same shape as #1: a serving-engine-side masking-overhead fix, benchmarked
   on multi-request cloud serving infrastructure (SGLang/vLLM), not on-device.
   **Verdict: differentiate-from** — same cluster, same reasoning; also worth
   one sentence in a future related-work section since "near-zero overhead"
   is a claim reviewers may reflexively raise against G1's tax finding, and
   the answer is "that's measured in a batched-server context we don't have
   on a phone/Jetson."
3. **The Speedup Paradox: Rethinking Inference Speed-Quality Trade-off in
   Embodied Tasks** (arXiv 2606.28529) — proposes TISED, an analytical
   framework for lossy inference optimizations (not grammar-masking
   specifically — quantization/pruning-style speedups) on embodied
   robot tasks. Core finding: task-level effects are non-monotonic (a
   "sweet spot" in optimization strength), and **that sweet spot's location
   shifts with hardware configuration**. Not competitive with G1 (different
   optimization class, no constrained decoding), but structurally the same
   shape of claim G1 is building toward — "an inference-time optimization's
   net effect depends on the device it runs on, not just the model." Useful
   as an independent-support citation if Route B (robot-grammar-under-
   deadline) is ever revived, and as general framing ammunition regardless of
   route: hardware-dependent optimization effects are an established, citable
   pattern in the embodied-AI literature, not something we're inventing.
   **Verdict: cite** (framing support, not a competitor).
