## Part 1 — Analyzing 005/006 (Stage-4 priority; results landed overnight, outside this loop's tracking)

Two experiments completed on Maria's Mac overnight 2026-08-12/13 and were committed
directly (`cbb635c`, `d8be373`, `28bd524`) with a full write-up already in
`experiments/results/NIGHT-REPORT-2026-08-13.md`. This section is this loop's read

**005 (short-forward-pass, 480/480 runs) — G1's mechanism is SUPPORTED for the first
time.** Paired (gbnf − unconstrained) tax, n=60/model, all four sub-1B models:

| model | decode speed | tax | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|
| qwen2.5:0.5b | 11.65 ms/tok | +1.84% | excludes zero |
| qwen3:0.6b | 13.71 | +2.42% | excludes zero |
| llama3.2:1b | 25.08 | +6.15% | excludes zero |
| gemma3:1b | 19.77 | **+30.36%** | excludes zero |

Every model below ~25 ms/token shows a positive tax with CI excluding zero — the
first time in five pilot rounds this has happened. Above ~34 ms/token (003, 004),
the tax was ≤1.8% and often not significant. **That is the crossover G1 predicted.**

**But it complicates the paper, it doesn't simplify it.** Absolute mask cost spans
28× (0.214ms → 6.002ms) across models decoding in the *same* 11–25ms band, and the
relative tax is not monotone in decode speed — gemma3:1b (19.77 ms/tok, faster than
llama3.2:1b) pays the largest tax by an order of magnitude. **Model identity
dominates decode speed.** The report's own candidate explanation is vocabulary size
(mask construction is O(vocab); see Part 2 — the general grammar-decoding literature
supports this as mechanistically plausible), with grammar-stack depth and tokenizer
fragmentation as alternatives — all three untested on this hardware. This is the
open thread, not a footnote: **without identifying the driver, G1 cannot claim "a
crossover exists" as a clean single-number result**, only "a crossover exists and
its size is model-specific for reasons not yet isolated."

The report also re-derives a generation-length confound (KV growth raises per-token
cost, and conditions don't generate equal token counts) and shows the qualitative
result survives it — qwen2.5:0.5b needs no correction (cleanest number, +1.8%
stands), llama3.2:1b roughly halves to ~+3.4%, gemma3:1b is untouched (~+29%),
qwen3:0.6b's tax is if anything understated. Solid, but explicitly "back-of-envelope
two-bucket slopes, not a fitted model" — a real regression is still undone.

**006 (qwen3 budget 768→3072)** did not fix qwen3's truncation (17/20 still hit the
new 3072 cap after ~2931 tokens of reasoning prose) — its −6.96% paired number is
the length confound in its most extreme form and the report correctly flags it as
**not usable** as a speed comparison. What it does establish cleanly: 20/20 vs 1/20
validity and a 5.8× energy gap (179.5 J vs 1046.0 J) driven by non-termination, not
decoding mechanics. qwen3 needs thinking disabled or a length-matched design before
it can enter the latency analysis.

**Validity in 005:** unconstrained scored 0/60 on all four models (no exceptions);
constrained scored 60/60, 60/60, 58/60, 52/60. This confirms sub-1B models do not
reliably self-produce the medical tool-call envelope unprompted — a real, useful
result independent of the tax question — and it is not the enum-case artifact
(fixed in `b64b4ed`, verified separately).

**Verdict: cite/build-on.** This is the strongest data point the G1 investigation
has produced. It is also, on its own, not yet a committable paper claim — see Part 3
for why this loop is opening a gate rather than queuing the obvious next experiment
and moving on as usual.

**Read in full (via search, since `arxiv.org` is also now blocked for direct fetch
in this session — abstract + methodology reconstructed from indexed excerpts, not a
PDF read): "The Constraint Tax" (arXiv 2605.26128).** This is the pre-read Maria's
loop flagged as needed *before* 005 ran (iteration 5's next action, point 2) — 005
ran first, so this is now a retrospective check, not a preventive one.

Methodology: 15,000 generations, Qwen2.5-0.5B / Qwen2.5-1.5B / SmolLM2-1.7B; hard
answer-only schema decoding raises validity 61.5%→100.0% but *drops* answer
accuracy 19.7%→11.0% and raises "wrong-but-valid" outputs 49.5%→88.9%. **The
directly relevant data point: on a deterministic calendar tool-call task,
Qwen2.5-1.5B holds 91.5% executable accuracy under prompt-only JSON but only 48.0%
under the equivalent hard tool-call schema — while both conditions are 100.0%
schema-valid.** That is the same task shape as G1's medical tool-call envelope, one
of the same model families (Qwen2.5) at a size 005 also used (0.5B).

**Confound assessment for 005/006: real and unaddressed.** 005/006 grade
constrained-vs-unconstrained purely on JSON-schema validity (60/60 vs 0/60, etc.).
Per Constraint Tax, a model can go from 0% to 100% schema-valid while its *answer
correctness* on the underlying task collapses — and G1's own numbers cannot
currently distinguish "the model is correctly filling in the medical envelope" from
"the model is emitting schema-valid nonsense it would not have produced
unconstrained." **This is the same shape of confound as the enum-case bug already
in CLAUDE.md's known-bugs list (validity metric silently dominated by something
other than genuine task performance) — but this time affecting the *constrained*
arm, not the unconstrained one, and it is unaddressed in the harness, not fixed.**
005/006's validity and tax numbers are internally consistent and the length-drift
correction is sound, but neither report can currently rule out that some fraction
of the 60/60-valid constrained runs are schema-valid junk. Verdict: **differentiate-
from / methodology gap to close**, not a kill — G1's mechanism claim (latency tax)
is independent of this, but any paper claim resembling "constrained decoding is
good/free/better here" needs a correctness axis the harness doesn't have yet.

**Related, already logged 2026-08-10, worth re-surfacing given 005's tool-call
domain:** "Constraint Tax in Open-Weight LLMs: Tool Calling Suppression Under
Structured Output Constraints" (arXiv 2606.25605) — finds that combining tool-
calling with JSON-schema constraints can make tool-call tokens unreachable under
the compiled grammar ("Constraint Priority Inversion"). Different mechanism from
both G1 (latency) and the correctness-tax paper above (answer accuracy), same
family of risk: constraints doing something other than what the schema author
intended. Not a scoop (no latency/energy angle), but worth a related-work line and
worth checking G1's grammar doesn't have this failure mode (unlikely — the medical
envelope's tool selection is fixed per-agent, not chosen from several tools — but
unverified).

**Mechanism-candidate check (vocab size):** general grammar-decoding literature
confirms per-step mask cost is `O(vocab)` in the naive case (dense logits scored
against every vocab token each step), and multiple 2026 papers target exactly this
(GPU-tensorized constraint automata, cited previously as PSC/GRID-adjacent). This
is consistent with — not proof of — the report's vocab-size hypothesis for gemma3's
outlier 30% tax (gemma3 is known for an unusually large vocabulary vs the Qwen/
Llama models in the same test). Still untested on this data; `n_vocab` was not
extractable from llama-server's logs per the report.

**llama.cpp novelty check (quick):** llama.cpp's grammar engine masks logits by
walking the full vocabulary against the grammar state each sampling step, on CPU,
inside the sampler chain — no evidence found of overlapping mask computation with
the GPU forward pass the way SGLang and vLLM are reported to (both overlap per-step
mask generation with LLM inference to hide grammar latency). If accurate, this
supports the novelty of "pipeline the mask across the CPU/GPU sync boundary"
specifically **for llama.cpp** — the engine Maria's assets actually use — even
though other engines have already explored similar territory. Not a scoop; a
reason the target-engine framing (not "constrained decoding in general") still
matters for the pitch.
