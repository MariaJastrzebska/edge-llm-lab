## Axis 2 — On-device visual/VLM inference for robots → **DEMOTE (keep as evidence)**

- **Seeing is Free, Speaking is Not: Uncovering the True Energy Bottleneck in
  Edge VLM Inference** — https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09520
  RTX 3070 Laptop + Jetson Orin NX (15 W); InternVL3-1B/2B, Qwen2-VL-2B,
  Qwen2.5-VL-3B, Gemma-3-4B. **Finding: output token generation is 86–97% of
  total energy; each output token costs 11–39× an input (incl. visual) token;
  removing ALL visual tokens saves ≤10%, while controlling output length saves
  up to 97%.** This is the single most important source of the sweep — see below.
- **vla.cpp: A Unified Inference Runtime for Vision-Language-Action Models**
  — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.08094 (the llama.cpp-for-VLA slot is taken)

- **Latency-Tolerant Cloud-Edge Collaborative VLA** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00569
- **Action-aware Dynamic Pruning for Efficient VLA** — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.22093
- **VLMs on the Edge for Real-Time Robotic Perception** — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.14921
- **Vision-Language Models for Edge Networks** — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.07855

from "what the robot sees" to "what happens per output token" — which is axis 3.

## The surviving gap — G1 (falsifiable)

Supporting sources on the decoding-machinery side:
- **XGrammar** — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.15100 · **XGrammar-2** (agentic)
  — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.04426 · blog: https://blog.mlc.ai/2024/11/22/achieving-efficient-flexible-portable-structured-generation-with-xgrammar
  Claim: **<40 µs/token, "near-zero overhead."** The mechanism of that claim is
  explicit: *the mask is computed on the CPU **in parallel with the GPU's forward
  pass***, precomputing context-independent tokens and live-checking the rest.
- **JSONSchemaBench** — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.10868
- **Guided decoding performance on vLLM/SGLang** (SqueezeBits)
  — https://blog.squeezebits.com/guided-decoding-performance-vllm-sglang
- **Constraint Tax in Open-Weight LLMs: Tool Calling Suppression Under
  Structured Output Constraints** — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.25605
  Measures the **accuracy** tax of constraints on tool calling. Explicitly *not*
  latency or energy, and not on edge hardware.
- **Copy-as-Decode: Grammar-Constrained Parallel Prefill** — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.18170
- **Constrained Decoding for Robotics Foundation Models (SafeDec)**
  — https://arxiv.org/html/2509.01728v3 — enforces STL safety specs on action
  trajectories at inference time, asserting **"minimal overhead"** (unquantified,
  not on edge, no energy).
- **When Correct Isn't Usable: Structured Output Reliability in SLMs**
  — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.02363
- **Small Models, Big Tasks: SLMs for Function Calling** — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.19277
- **llama.cpp GBNF docs** — https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/grammars/README.md
  Acknowledges **"performance gotchas… may result in extremely slow sampling"**
  for deep token stacks — **with no quantified numbers anywhere.**
- Counter-evidence to the "free" folklore: reports of constrained decoding at
  **3.6×–8.2× naive-decoding latency** on GSM8K-class workloads (surfaced via
  the structured-decoding overhead sweep) — i.e. the literature *contradicts
  itself* by two orders of magnitude, unresolved.

### G1 statement
> **No published work measures the latency *and energy* cost of grammar-
> constrained / schema-constrained decoding on edge SoCs — where the CPU-side
> mask construction cannot be hidden behind a long GPU forward pass, and where
> CPU and GPU contend for the same unified-memory bandwidth. All "near-zero
> overhead" results are obtained on server GPUs with large models, under an
> overlap assumption that a 0.5–3B model on Apple Silicon / mobile / Pi-class
> hardware structurally violates.**

**Why it is still open.** Measuring it requires three instruments in one rig that
normally live in different communities: (a) per-phase runtime instrumentation of
the sampler, (b) device-level energy telemetry, (c) structured tool-call fidelity
scoring. Decoding-systems groups have (a); energy groups have (b); agent-eval
groups have (c). The overlap assumption was never revisited when the field moved
from A100s to phones — it was inherited as folklore.

**Why it matters (and why it is not a benchmark paper).** 2607.09520 shows decode
is 86–97% of edge inference energy. Constrained decoding perturbs decode in two
opposing directions at once: it **adds** per-token CPU work on the critical path,
and it **removes** tokens by forbidding malformed/verbose output. The net sign of
that on Joules-per-*valid-tool-call* is unknown and unmeasured. If the tax is
real, the fix is a mechanism, and the mechanism is upstreamable low-level work in
llama.cpp: move mask construction off the critical path (build step *t+1*'s mask
during step *t*'s GEMV), avoid the vocab-sized CPU↔GPU sync that unified memory
makes deceptively cheap-looking, persist/incrementalize the grammar stack.

**Assets reused:** edge-llm-lab telemetry backends (powermetrics / RAPL /
tegratstats), `_parse_llama_timings` in the llama-server runtime, existing
tool-call fidelity metrics, Apple Silicon Macs + iPhone 16 Pro (fllama = same
llama.cpp core), Maria's stated intent to contribute to llama.cpp.

### Secondary gaps (parked, not dead)
- **G2** — Energy-per-*valid*-output as a first-class metric. Every energy paper
  above reports J/token or tokens/watt; in an agentic setting a structurally
  invalid call costs full energy and delivers zero work. Retries are invisible in
  J/token. Cheap to add to our schema, and it reframes the ~3.5-BPW threshold
  result (2505.15030) in a way nobody has.
- **G3** — Real-time guarantees: constrained decoding changes not just mean
  latency but its *tail*, and a robot control loop is a deadline system. SafeDec
  asserts minimal overhead without a distribution. No one reports p99 for
  constrained decode on edge.
