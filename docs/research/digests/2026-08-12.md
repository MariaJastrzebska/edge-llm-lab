## Part 1 — Analysis of NIGHT-REPORT-2026-08-12.md (enum-fixed remeasure + 004)

### The bug fix worked, partially
Granite 002, enum-fixed vs confounded: unconstrained schema-validity rose
1/100 → 18/100 (still nowhere near the constrained conditions' 100/100). J-per-valid
gap shrank from ~100× to ~5.7×. Residual unconstrained failures are now diverse
(type errors, missing properties, `anyOf` failures) — no single artifact
dominates. One residual failure is a correct-behavior-punished case: a prompt
says "I'm 310cm tall," the model faithfully records it, and the schema (max
250) correctly rejects it — a scoring-policy question, not a measurement bug.

### Decode time is not comparable across nights — confirmed, mechanism proposed
Same model/build/flags/prompts decoded at 41.90ms/token (08-10), 36.83
(08-11), 34.64 (08-12) — monotonically faster over three nights. Idle-baseline
power went the *opposite* direction (lowest on the slowest night), ruling out
thermal drift. Leading hypothesis: **observer contention** — heavy polling/
git/editing on the same 8-core M1 during 08-10's run, near-total hands-off
during 08-12's. Consistent with the data, not proven (no A/B run). **Only
within-run paired comparisons are valid**; the paired design (originally for
thermal drift) turns out load-bearing for a different confound.

**Reading this honestly:** phi4-mini's three independent 08-12 intervals all
agree on ~+1.5–1.8%, the closest thing to a real reproducible structured-
generation latency tax found so far. Granite's apparent effect (present in
002 and 003, both CIs excluding zero) evaporated in 004's larger-n run —
the report itself flags this as likely "ordinary consequence of running many
comparisons near the resolution limit," not a detection, and explicitly
declines to cherry-pick the 003 number. qwen3's negative sign remains an

so its "faster" constrained condition is not decoding comparable material —

### What this does and doesn't mean for G1
Magnitude: **~1.5–1.8%, not the 3.6–8.2× the literature's high end claims** —
consistent with iteration 3/4's reading, now on firmer footing (replicated,
not just "not detected"). Mechanism: **still untested where G1 predicts the
effect is largest** — nothing measured has decoded faster than ~34.6ms/token,
still well inside the regime where a CPU mask can hide behind the GPU forward
pass. Energy: phi4-mini's constrained conditions cost *more* J (155 vs
139) while granite's cost *less* (118–119 vs 121) — tracks token count, not
per-token cost (grammar changes how much these models write, not measured
independently of the latency effect). Converter equivalence (Python gbnf vs
llama-server's internal json_schema→gbnf) held again on a second model:
identical token counts, times within 0.11ms.

### PSC / mask-cost-erosion scoop re-check — no kill, two new adjacent papers found
Re-ran the "algorithmic mask speedup + CPU/GPU pipelining + Joules + edge
hardware" combination query — still nothing measures pipelined mask overlap
in Joules on short-forward-pass edge hardware. Two new algorithmic-mask-speedup
papers found, same erosion-risk bucket as PSC (shrink the ceiling, don't touch
the sync-stall mechanism):
- **GRID: Grammar-Railed Decoding for Enterprise SQL Generation** —
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11951 — keys next-token masks on LALR(1) parser
  configuration instead of token sequences; claims >2× lower mask latency than
  XGrammar on complex grammars. Enterprise SQL domain, no edge-hardware or
  energy measurement, condensed version under review at KDD 2027. **Verdict:
  differentiate-from, erosion-risk (same bucket as PSC).**
- **Attention Meets Reachability: Structural Equivalence and Efficiency in
  Grammar-Constrained LLM Decoding** — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.05540 —
  another algorithmic mask-computation speedup via structural equivalence.
  No edge/energy angle found in search snippets. **Verdict: differentiate-from,
  erosion-risk (same bucket).**

### Adjacent but different-axis: two "tax" papers on the validity side, not latency/energy
- **The Constraint Tax: Measuring Validity-Correctness Tradeoffs in
  Structured Outputs for Small Language Models** —
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26128 — Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B, SmolLM2-1.7B,
  15k generations: hard schema decoding raises validity 61.5%→100% but *drops*
  answer accuracy 19.7%→11.0%. **Verdict: cite** — directly adjacent to the
  002/003/004 validity numbers (different axis: correctness cost, not
  latency/energy cost), and on exactly the sub-1B/sub-2B model class queued
  experiment 005 targets. Worth reading in full before 005 runs — it may mean
  005's small models produce *valid-but-wrong* JSON under constraint, a
  confound the current pilot's grading might not distinguish from "correct."
- **The Format Tax** — https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03616 — format-*requesting
  instructions* (not decoder constraints) cause most of a reasoning/accuracy
  degradation across open-weight models; closed-weight models show little of
  it. **Verdict: weak-cite** — different mechanism (prompt-level, not decode-
  time), but useful caution that some of any measured "constraint effect"
  could be prompt-framing rather than the grammar itself; worth one sentence
  in a future draft's threats-to-validity section.
- **Decode-Time Grammars** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18357 — runtime-
  environment-typed grammar fragments for code-gen correctness (undeclared
  variables, missing schema columns). **Verdict: ignore** — semantic-grounding
  problem, not the latency/energy mechanism G1 is about.
