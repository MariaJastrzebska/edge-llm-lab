## Correctness-tax cluster — four new-to-`seen.json` items, two data-hygiene fixes

1. **VAREX: A Benchmark for Multi-Modal Structured Extraction from Documents**
   (arXiv 2603.15118, IBM Research) — **differentiate-from/cite, headline.**
   1,777 government-form documents, 1,771 unique JSON schemas, four input
   modalities, ~98.5% field-level ground-truth accuracy via a three-phase QA
   process. Headline finding: **below 4B parameters, structured-output
   compliance — not extraction capability — is the dominant bottleneck**;
   "schema echo" (the model emitting schema-conforming structure instead of
   extracted values) depresses scores by **45–65 percentage points** in
   affected models, and extraction-specific fine-tuning at 2B recovers +81pp.
   This is the second correctness-tax finding this loop has logged that is
   scoped specifically to Maria's own sub-4B regime (joins "Repair, Not
   Improvement" / tool-call abstention, iter 49) and the sharpest statement
   yet of *why* schema-validity and task-correctness diverge most at small
   scale — a small model can satisfy the grammar by echoing it rather than
   filling it. Cloud/frontier-model evaluation methodology, no edge-hardware
   or latency/energy angle, no grammar-mask-cost mechanism — not a scoop.
2. **ExtractBench: A Benchmark and Evaluation Methodology for Complex
   Structured Extraction** (arXiv 2602.12247) — **differentiate-from/cite.**
   35 PDF documents paired with JSON Schemas and human-annotated gold labels
   (12,867 evaluatable fields) across five high-value domains; schema-driven
   per-field scoring (exact match / numeric tolerance / semantic equivalence).
   Finding: frontier models (OpenAI/Anthropic/Google) hit **0% valid output**
   on a 369-field financial-reporting schema, and on one domain **90% valid
   output but only 12.5% pass rate** — another independent domain confirming
   the cluster's central schema-valid-≠-correct claim, this time via breadth
   (field count) rather than reasoning load as the stressor. Cloud-API
   models, no edge angle.
3. **ExtractBench: A Benchmark for Schema-Guided Enterprise Document
   Extraction** (arXiv 2607.29677, LlamaIndex) — **differentiate-from/cite,
   name-collision flag.** A *second, unrelated* paper sharing the exact name
   "ExtractBench" with item 2 above — distinct arXiv ID, distinct authors
   (LlamaIndex vs. item 2's team), distinct dataset (370 enterprise documents/
   4,869 pages/67 document types vs. item 2's 35-PDF/5-domain set), distinct
   metrics (value F1 + grounding + cost vs. item 2's per-field exact/
   tolerance/semantic scoring). Both are genuinely on-cluster (schema-guided
   extraction, valid-vs-correct gap), so both are kept, but flagged plainly
   — same class of risk as the standing ATLAS/HARBOR/G1 name collisions — to
   stop a future sweep from treating a hit on "ExtractBench" as confirmation
   of either paper already being on file. Commercial VLM/coding-agent
   evaluation, no edge angle.
4. **Prompt Complexity Dilutes Structured Reasoning: A Follow-Up Study on the
   Car Wash Problem** (arXiv 2603.13351) — **ignore, filed to avoid
   re-discovery.** Despite the keyword overlap ("structured reasoning"), this
   is not a schema/grammar-constrained-decoding paper at all — it tests
   whether a reasoning *framework* (STAR) embedded in a 60+-line production
   prompt (a mock interview-coaching assistant) survives being surrounded by
   competing instructions (degrades from 85–100% in isolation to 0–30% in
   context on Claude Sonnet 4.6). A different sense of "structured" entirely
   (reasoning scaffolding, not output schema validity) — same keyword-overlap
   risk class as iter 49's Projectional-Decoding and Semantic-Probabilistic-
   Control filings.

**Two data-hygiene fixes** (same class as iters 22/29/31/32/33/36/38/41/42/

in `seen.json` despite being genuinely on-cluster:
- **arXiv 2604.03616, "The Format Tax"** (bare since 2026-08-12/2026-08-20,
  two separate bare entries for `/html/` and `/abs/` forms — a second,
  smaller instance of the standing URL-format-duplicate issue, see below) —
  now annotated. Decomposes the structured-output accuracy cost into a
  **prompt-level cost (introduced by the format-requesting instruction
  itself) and a decoder-level cost (introduced by constrained-decoding token
  masking)** across ten models and four formats (JSON/XML/LaTeX/Markdown):
  instructing JSON costs ~-3.9 points on average, enforcing it at decode
  time costs a further ~-1.6 points, and **the prompt-level cost dominates**
  — closed-weight models largely resist the tax, open-weight models don't.
  This is a precise, quantified version of the correctness-tax gap this
  section has tracked since iter 7, and the prompt-vs-decoder decomposition
  is directly reusable methodology for the harness-OS paper's own
  correctness-grading-gate design (joins "Where vs What"/SCD, iter 41, and
  the Structural-vs-Semantic template, iter 45, as reusable eval-design
  citations). No edge-hardware or latency/energy angle.
